> **StockPets** lets beginners live through about 30 years of US stock market history, starting in January 1996, one month at a time with virtual coins. It doesn’t teach you how to make money or predict anything. Instead, it lets you experience patience, spreading crops across plots, drawdowns and recoveries, and delisting risk in a low-stakes, cozy way.

## Can a game really teach you about the stock market?

It depends on what you mean by “teach.” A game can’t tell you what to buy, and StockPets never tries. It doesn’t offer investment advice, it doesn’t claim to show you how to make money, and it never implies that past returns predict future ones.

What a game can do is let you experience something you’d otherwise only read about. Charts compress decades into a single picture. In StockPets, you live through those decades month by month, with a pet beside you and a farm whose crops follow historical US stock prices. The difference between seeing a long decline on a chart and sitting through it one turn at a time is the whole point.

## Time is the main ingredient

One turn is one month. During your first 12 months you can move as fast as you like, but after that, one calendar page refills every 8 hours, and you can hold up to 3. That rhythm is a quiet lesson in itself. You can’t fast-forward through a bad stretch, and you can’t rush a good one.

It also means your relationship with time feels more like real life. Thirty years is a long time when you experience it month by month, and the game makes that obvious in a way a chart never does. The good news: nothing decays while you’re away, so patience doesn’t cost you anything except waiting.

## Several plots, not one big bet

Your farm starts with 3 plots and can grow to 11. Each plot holds a different crop, and each crop follows a different company’s price. Over time you notice that crops rarely move in perfect step. One month a single plot has a great run; another month it’s the one dragging everything down.

The market weather line makes this visible: it appears when the average move of all your crops in a month goes beyond ±3.5%. Seeing an average next to individual crops is a simple, hands-on way to understand what people mean by diversification. The game isn’t recommending a strategy; it just lets you see how a mix behaves.

## Drawdowns and recoveries feel different from the inside

Starting in 1996 means you’ll live through the late-1990s internet boom, the 2000–2002 bust, the 2008 financial crisis, the long recovery of the 2010s and the sharp crash and rebound of 2020. Reading about these is one thing. Turning the page month after month while your crops shrink is another.

StockPets softens this with care rather than punishment. Losses can lead your pet to the Scruffy or Raincoat look when it grows up, but market losses never kill your pet. That makes it a gentle place to notice how you feel during a drawdown. Read more in [how your pet grows](/stockpets/guides/pet-growth/).

## Companies can disappear

In StockPets, when a company on your farm is delisted, that crop dies and its game value becomes zero — even when the delisting comes from an acquisition or merger. That’s stricter than real life, where shareholders of an acquired company may receive cash or shares, and the game says so plainly: it’s a deliberate rule meant to make the risk simple and memorable.

Across the whole archive there are more than 800 companies, and more of them join the list over the years. Watching companies arrive and, sometimes, vanish is one of the clearest things the game shows about how markets change. Our guide on [what happens when a stock is delisted](/stockpets/guides/what-happens-when-a-stock-is-delisted/) explains what typically happens in the real world.

## You only ever see the past

Each seed shows its “price history so far,” never the months ahead. You vaguely remember the broad shape of the future, but not the details. That’s a neat way to feel hindsight bias: in the moment, nothing is as obvious as it looks in a history book. If this part interests you, [going back to 1996](/stockpets/guides/back-to-1996/) is all about it.

## The emotional side of watching a market

The game is built to be calm. Each month brings a small life event — a pigeon at the window, the first snow, cocoa, fireflies — and a memory of your room, pet and farm is saved to the Album. Those small, ordinary moments sit right next to the market weather, which helps put a stormy month in perspective.

## What StockPets won’t teach you

It’s worth being honest about the limits. StockPets is a game about history, not a trading tool:

- Coins are virtual and can’t be deposited, withdrawn or exchanged for money or prizes.
- There are no real trades, no cash dividends, no taxes and no fees.
- Prices are monthly closes adjusted for stock splits only, and each company starts at 100 coins in its first recorded month. See [how prices and splits work](/stockpets/guides/stock-split-explained-split-adjusted-prices/).
- It’s not investment advice, and past returns don’t predict future ones.
- It doesn’t follow live prices; it plays out historical months, one turn at a time.

Those limits are part of why it works as a starting point. With virtual coins rather than real stock trades, your decisions shape your farm’s story and your pet’s grown look. You can make a choice, live with it for a few years of game time and see what happens, then reflect on how it felt. If you ever want to start fresh, “Start over” in Settings lets you begin a new journey, and “Export my records” lets you keep a copy of the journey you just finished.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is StockPets good for stock market beginners?

StockPets is a cozy way to experience decades of real US stock market history with virtual coins. It’s not a course or investment advice, but it lets beginners feel things like patience, drawdowns and delisting risk firsthand.

### Does StockPets use real stock market data?

Yes. Seeds follow historical US monthly closing prices, adjusted for stock splits, with each company’s first recorded month set to 100 coins.

### Can I lose real money playing StockPets?

No real stocks are traded. The game’s investment coins are virtual and cannot be deposited, withdrawn or exchanged for cash.

### Will StockPets make me a better investor?

StockPets doesn’t promise that, and it never suggests past returns predict the future. It’s meant to let you experience market history, not to teach a way to make money.

### Which market events will I live through in StockPets?

Starting in 1996, you’ll play through the late-1990s internet boom, the 2000–2002 bust, the 2008 financial crisis, the recovery of the 2010s and the 2020 pandemic crash and rebound.

StockPets uses virtual coins only. This article is not investment advice, and past price changes do not predict future results.
