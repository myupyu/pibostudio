Begin at Grandma’s shop. Choose the goods to bring in, set a price, and prepare to welcome customers. When the day ends, read the ledger: what sold, and what remains? Seoul Alley 1988 starts with that small sequence.

## The numbers beyond sales

More sales do not automatically leave the same amount of extra cash. Stock costs money. So does running the shop, and the household needs its share. Preparing for another day means reading the other lines of the ledger, too.

Managing the shop does not follow a single path toward the biggest building. You choose whether to keep its current scale, prepare another space, or change the way it operates.

## The same alley, changing lives

As time passes, people’s circumstances change alongside the street. Conversations with family and neighbors carry something of the years they live in. Earlier words and actions leave traces in later scenes and memories.

That gives you a reason to remember a promise as well as a purchase. A conversation after closing can become part of deciding how to run the next day.

## Start by learning the flow

Prepare the stock, look at prices, open for business, and check what remains. Then plan what comes next. [Reading the shop alongside its ledger](/seoul-alley-1988/guides/read-your-first-ledger/) gathers a few things to notice at the beginning.
