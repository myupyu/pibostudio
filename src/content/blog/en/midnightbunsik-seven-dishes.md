The menu at Midnight Bunsik has seven dishes: tteokbokki, gimbap, fish cake, corn dogs, ramyeon, hotteok, and sikhye. Each asks for its own little gestures as you place ingredients and cook.

## Keep an order slip beside you

Begin by checking who you are cooking for and what they asked for. Follow the cues in the cooking scene, work with its ingredients and tools, choose a finishing touch, and serve. The food looks different while being prepared and after it is finished.

## Leave a small choice on the plate

A finishing touch changes the illustration on the plate. When heat suits the dish, choose its level directly and read the guest’s reaction afterward. Even a familiar favorite can become a different meal.

## Quickly, or gently

Some nights are for hands-on preparation. Others are for staying a little longer with the story and the table. Quick and assisted cooking, relaxed timing, and calm controls offer different ways through. Buying convenience tools is not a requirement for continuing the story.

## The response belongs to the meal

Finishing the cooking does not immediately push you toward the next order. Serving and reading the response afterward are part of the meal, too. Notice a well-prepared dish and an imperfect one; the guest’s story continues in either case.

If the cooking scene is new to you, read [the first-meal guide](/midnightbunsik/guides/first-meal/).
