Once the rest of the alley has gone to sleep, the light comes on in a little Korean snack shop. You’re a reaper in training, learning to run the shop from Granny and cooking for the guests who find their way to the counter. How spicy does this customer like their tteokbokki? Who do they think of when they eat gimbap? After a few visits, you begin to hear the stories behind their orders.

## Nine dishes to make by hand

Reduce the sauce for tteokbokki and choose how much chili to add. Roll the gimbap and slice it into eight pieces. Pour broth over a fishcake skewer or put ice in a cup of sikhye. The menu also includes ramyeon, hotteok, and Korean corn dogs. Pan-fry and steam mandu, or toss bibim noodles until the pale strands are coated in sauce. A dish that’s underdone or unevenly seasoned looks different on the plate, and guests notice.

You can retry a step on your first plate. With assisted cooking turned on, you can choose when to take a pan off the heat or lift the lid using buttons. The story continues after an imperfect meal. Browse [all nine dishes](/midnightbunsik/blog/seven-dishes/) or follow the [first meal guide](/midnightbunsik/guides/first-meal/).

## The first 21 nights, and the neighbors who stay

Over the first 21 nights, you learn the names and tastes of returning customers. In some scenes you can choose where a keepsake goes or who gets a share. Those decisions can leave something on the table and shape what a guest says when they come back. A carefully cooked meal and a clumsy first attempt can bring out different sides of the same customer.

After the main story, you get to know the neighbors who are still in the alley. There are new meals to serve and memories to find tucked away on the shelves. You can keep opening the shop after the follow-up story ends whenever you feel like it. [A table that remembers](/midnightbunsik/blog/a-table-that-remembers/) goes into how those choices work without giving away the endings.

## What we’ve been working on before launch

We’ve refined Walt’s first meal, Granny’s order, and the scenes where guests share a table. We made room for all three finishing choices on smaller screens and smoothed out dialogue breaks in Korean and English. We also fixed an older save path that could skip the first follow-up chapter on night 22, and checked what happens when you reopen the app or finish a purchase.

The game supports Korean and English. You can play offline with no account, with larger text and reduced motion available in Settings. Progress stays on your device, and you can export it before moving to another phone. See [how to save and return to your shop](/midnightbunsik/guides/save-and-return/).

We’re preparing Midnight Bunsik for iOS and Android. The main story and follow-up are free, with optional one-time ad removal planned. Public store links will appear on the [game page](/midnightbunsik/) as they become available.
