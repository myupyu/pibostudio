A day in Jangdok Garden begins among rows of fermentation jars. Before preparing another batch, look at yesterday’s food. Is it ready to take out, or does it need more time? Then turn to your ingredients and the orders waiting at the workshop.

## Between making food and selling it

Adding ingredients is a beginning. Kimchi, jang, pickles, and vinegar need their fermentation checked. Taking a finished batch out and putting it on the shelf is part of the same job.

Making more food and tending a workshop well ask different questions. What can you sell today? What will be ready later? What should you prepare next? Those questions give the day its shape.

## Look at the weather and the orders

The courtyard’s weather is part of caring for your jars. Check their condition and decide what needs attention, while keeping neighbors’ requests in mind. A new day gives you a reason to reconsider which job comes first.

An order is also a reminder that someone lives beyond the workshop. A sale can end while a conversation, a letter, or a promise carries on.

## Leave room for a letter

Grandma’s letters and the neighbors’ stories sit alongside the work. There is time to read between batches, and the direction you choose for the workshop grows beside those conversations.

You do not need to memorize every food at once. Begin with the path from preparing a batch to checking it, taking it out, and sharing it. [A guide to the first days](/jangdokgarden/guides/first-days/) gathers a few things to notice.
